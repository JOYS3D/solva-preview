04The question you never planned for

And when they ask
something you never covered?

This is the question every owner asks, and the one every vendor avoids. So here is the honest answer.

The caller asks

“Do you do the windows on the second floor as well? We’ve had trouble finding someone with the right ticket.”

Nothing in the brief covers working at height. Three things happen, in this order.

  1. 01

    It says it doesn’t know.

    It never guesses, never improvises a yes, and never invents a price. That single rule is what keeps it from costing you a job it was never able to do.

    “That one I’m not sure about — I don’t want to give you the wrong answer. Let me get it confirmed for you.”
  2. 02

    It writes the question down.

    Word for word, attached to the caller’s details. You read what they actually asked, not somebody’s summary of it.

  3. 03

    It follows your rule.

    You decide in advance: put them through to you if you’re free, promise a callback within the hour, or simply flag it for the morning. Different rules for different hours if you want.

Then it stops being a surprise.

You answer the question once. From that call onwards, it answers it the same way — in your words, because they are your words.

First call

“I’ll get that confirmed for you.” Flagged to you.

You answer once

“Yes, up to two storeys. Above that we quote separately.”

Every call after

Answered directly. Still your sentence, still editable.

To be precise about what this is: nothing is being retrained. It is a list of your answers that grows as you correct it — you can read it, change it, or delete a line at any time.

06Straight answers

What it does.
And what it won’t.

The limits matter more than the promises. Anyone selling you a phone assistant that never needs a human is selling you a problem.

It will

  • Answer every call, at any hour, in your business’s name
  • Ask the questions you agreed, in your words
  • Work with the diary you already use, and never offer a time it cannot see is free
  • Write the booking straight into your calendar
  • Separate an emergency from a price enquiry
  • Say plainly that it’s an assistant, if asked
  • Hand you anything it couldn’t answer, word for word

It won’t

  • Quote a price you haven’t set
  • Book outside the rules you gave it — hours, job types, deposits
  • Improvise an answer it doesn’t have
  • Make outbound calls. Inbound only.
  • Replace the conversation a difficult customer needs with you
