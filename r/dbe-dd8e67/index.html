Finding 03 — the finder

Your parts finder isn't broken. It's starved.

Working

The code is fine — someone built it properly

The vehicle selector on your home page is a custom theme section (parts_finder_hero_v3). Four cascading menus, built deliberately. The v3 says it has been through at least three revisions.

It returns nothing because the products underneath it carry no vehicle tags. 453 products have no tags at all. Fix the data and the filter you already paid for starts working — no new development, no new app subscription.

Recoverable

Your titles already hold the answer

Twelve vehicle makes are already readable straight out of your product titles — Ford (201), Isuzu (100), Mitsubishi (71), Mazda (57), LDV (47), Toyota (46), Nissan (44), GWM (36) and more. The make, model and series a customer needs to filter by is sitting in the text; it has simply never been written into the tag field the finder reads.

That's why this is a mapping job, not a data-entry job.

Finding 05 — part numbers

Sixteen part numbers point at two different products

35 SKUs appear on more than one product page. 19 of those are simply the duplicate listings from Finding 06. The other 16 are real collisions, across 77 pages — the same part number on genuinely different products.

Ordering risk

The same number, two different vehicles

60XRBBench is the part number on Seat Adapter Kit to suit 40 Series Landcruiser and on Seat Adapter Kit to suit 60 Series Landcruiser. Four more seat adapter numbers do the same thing.

Whichever system you use to pick and pack, it cannot tell those two apart. Neither can a supplier order built from your SKUs.

Also

144 part numbers aren't part numbers

They're sentences. One reads N-RB1-FORD-01-BAR WORK FORD RANGER PX3 07/2018+. Another, AM106-LC300-L1 / AM106-LC300-L2 — two numbers in one field. A SKU has to be one short unique code, or nothing downstream can use it.

The fix

Six steps, in this order

Tax and weights first — those two touch money and postage every day they stay as they are.

01

Export and map

Full catalogue export, then build the make/model/series mapping table from your existing titles. Roughly 130 vehicle rows.

02

Script the repair

Tax, weights, vehicle tags, collections and SKUs written in one pass. Re-import, then verify against the export.

03

Merge the duplicates

235 titles reduced to one each, with a 301 redirect from every retired URL so the rankings follow.

04

Fold the split categories

44 categories down to 36. Menus and collection pages rebuilt around the merged set.

05

Add what's missing

The Hamer products absent from your store, against your current supplier list. Anything beyond Hamer is a separate piece of work.

06

Test and hand over

Finder, cart, shipping and checkout on desktop and mobile. Plus a short written record of what changed and how to add a product correctly yourself.

What this audit does not claim

We could multiply the $695,450 of untaxed catalogue by the GST rate and print a frightening number. We haven't, because it would assume every product sells exactly once. That's arithmetic, not a forecast, and presenting it as a loss would be the kind of claim section 18 of the Australian Consumer Law exists to stop.

What we've given you is what we measured. The catalogue value is a fact. What it costs you is a number only you can work out, because only you know what actually sells.
